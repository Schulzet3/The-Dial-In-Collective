## Distinct factual findings and primary-source limits

1. PDF pages 1-2: primary Kaffeemacher Slow Feeding report, Benjamin Hohlmann, May 3 2025, https://kaffeemacher.ch/en/blogs/kaffeewissen/slow-feeding/ original Methods, Table 1, extraction section. For tested DF64/Apas, normal versus same-setting manual slow feeding raised median measured particle size 250 to 299 micrometers and reduced fraction under 100 micrometers from 33.0% to 23.9%; Limontitla gave 229 to 255 micrometers and 33.4% to 28.4%. Grounds were analyzed with a ZHAW Camsizer X2. Two studies used DF64 Gen 1/2, 18g, ~50-70s slow-feed vs ~5-10s normal. Second experiment used 5 shots per condition. Grade: useful apparatus-specific first-party PSD and flow comparison, not cross-grinder replication or universal sensory preference.

2. PDF pages 2-3: the first-party Apas example about 25 to 10 seconds at unchanged grinder setting differed markedly from Limontitla 27 to 22 seconds. Re-adjusting grind/recipe to restore duration is not an isolated taste intervention. Original experimental numbers cannot prescribe a universal 30-50% grinder shift, guaranteed flavor benefit, or ideal espresso timing. Kaffeemacher itself calls for further sensory replication and used non-DIC descriptive 1-6 ratings; not DIC all-five next-shot outcomes.

3. PDF pages 2,4-6: independently re-read Jonathan Gagne original April 12 2019 first-party study https://coffeeadastra.com/2019/04/12/grind-quality-and-the-popcorning-effect/ . One Baratza Forte BG, one light Ethiopian coffee, 3 distinct 10g feeding conditions with 12 imaging samples each. Bean-by-bean particles were shifted, but ordinary empty-hopper single-dose vs full hopper were virtually indistinguishable at that dose; grinding-motor startup is an acknowledged unisolated factor. It is observational support for grinder/feed dependence, NOT independent DF64 replication, statistically proven flavor improvement or universal offset. Smrke et al 2024 fines/permeability publisher check was already accepted in closed #335: reuse, no duplicate review/source credit.
