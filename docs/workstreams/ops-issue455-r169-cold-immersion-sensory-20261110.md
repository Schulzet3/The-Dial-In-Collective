## Original primary study
Directly read original publisher [Batali et al., *Foods* (2022), DOI 10.3390/foods11162440](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/11/16/2440), Methods §§2.1–2.6, Results §§3.1–3.4, Discussion, Conclusions, funding and data availability. Original publisher HTML accessible, but PDF binary, figure pixel interpretation, supplements and individual respondent records were not acquired or hashed. Raw data available from author on request. **No claim of complete PDF/figures or lab-data replication.**

**Design and factual result:** 3 origins (one Ethiopia washed, one El Salvador honey, one Sumatra wet-hulled) x 3 roasting levels x 3 steep temperatures (4, 22 and 92 C) = 27 distinct full-immersion conditions, triplicate. 100g ground coffee/500g water in Toddy immersion to extraction equilibrium over hours, then **all diluted to 2% TDS and served at 4 C**. Panel start 20, 17 formal, 14 completed, four excluded for inter-replicate inconsistency, 10 analyzed trained participants. They used 27 descriptor scores on 0–100 lines, not personal DIC 1–5 scale. Reported across tested coffees: more sour/bitter/rubbery at 92 C and more floral at 4 C; different roasts/origins show interactions. Brew pH changed but titratable acidity generally did not match perceived sourness by temp.

**Limits:** This is **full immersion, not pressure espresso**. TDS was equalized, but extraction percentage differed, roasting/origin/process were not independently separated (one origin per process), extended hot brew may have lost aroma, training under COVID and lighting not standardized, small final panel, funding Coffee Science Foundation with Toddy LLC underwriting/equipment supplied. The compound-mechanism explanations are author hypotheses, not chromatographic demonstrations in this experiment. Their 4/22/92 C are not DIC espresso temperature recommendations. No measured DIC member ratings or improved cup outcomes claimed.
