## New substantive original-source verification (previously abstract only)

- Charles, Romano, Yener et al., *Understanding flavour perception of espresso coffee by the combination of a dynamic sensory method and in-vivo nosespace analysis*, *Food Research International* 69 (2015), pp9–20, DOI [10.1016/j.foodres.2014.11.036](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2014.11.036). Original article is **reprinted in coauthor Sine Yener's 2016 institutional doctoral thesis**, Chapter 4.1 printed pp112–137, [hosted by Fondazione Edmund Mach](https://openpub.fmach.it/retrieve/e1dbfeab-0086-4ac9-e053-1705fe0a1c61/Thesis_SINE%20YENER%20final.pdf); [original institution publication record](https://openpub.fmach.it/handle/10449/25273). Reprint and publisher article refer to **one experiment**, not an independent replication.
- Direct primary-article *parsed text*: complete introduction, samples and subject methods §§4.1.1–4.1.2, TDS protocol, nosespace PTR-ToF-MS processing, 4.1.3 quantitative descriptions, Discussion §4.1.4 and Conclusions §4.1.5 independently read. PDF screenshots were requested but failed source-cache retrieval; no claim that pages/figures were visually inspected. Original journal publisher PDF remains subscription restricted; thesis PDF binary hash, raw data and independent outcome replication **NOT VERIFIED**.
- Samples: 2 commercially naturally low-caffeine Illy Arabica Iperespresso capsule roast levels (light A, dark B) each with or without 100 mg/mL *added sucrose*. Iperespresso X7.1 brewed 30-mL cups, **10 mL crema-free, 55°C aliquots served through a lidded straw** to limit pre-mouth smell. Not ordinary uncontrolled, full-crema or no-added-sugar espresso preference testing. Eighteen adult institute-employee volunteers (10 women/8 men, 23–37 years), **only 3 with previous sensory analysis**, subsequently trained; four sample conditions × 3 repeats ×18 participants, 60-second TDS observations on **nine possible dominant descriptors** (not simultaneous independently rated intensities).
- Same participant nosespace PTR-ToF-MS was analyzed for temporal volatile release. Original reduction retains 39 mass peaks × six kinetics columns ×72 participant×coffee aggregates. Temporal dominance of sweet, caramel and nutty percepts changed markedly with *added sugar*, masking sour/bitter; author reports sucrose made little difference to instrumental in-vivo volatile release. Different roast level affected volatile intensities and sensory bitter/burnt/roasted dominance. Chemical kinetics do not establish causal sweetness corrections or numerical preference priors for naturally unsweetened member shots. Original experimental funding included Illycaffè; cross-brand generality and reproducible human cup liking were not demonstrated.
