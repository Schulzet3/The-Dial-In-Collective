**Primary source newly substantively reviewed:** Liang, Batali, Routt, Ristenpart, Guinard, *Sensory analysis of the flavor profile of full immersion hot, room temperature, and cold brewed coffee over time*, *Scientific Reports* 14:19298 (20 Aug 2024), DOI [10.1038/s41598-024-69867-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-69867-6), PMID 39164402, [direct full original NIH PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11335879/). Read original article Methods (experimental design, brewing, chemistry, sensory and stats), Results (including Table 2), Discussion, limitations, funding and data availability, not merely abstract. Study uses one Central-American blend roasted light and darker-end-medium-dark (2 levels), three brew-water temperatures (4/22/92 C), five timepoints each, 30 conditions. Preparation: 100 g/500 g coarse ~972±19µm grounds, nonpressurized full immersion, twofold beverage dilution, refrigerated overnight and evaluated cold (~6–12 C). Fourteen trained descriptive panelists gave triplicate 0–100 attribute intensities. TDS/TA/pH measured; 25 of 28 Results attributes varied among products. This is **not pressure espresso**, not a consumer-hedonic success measure and not a five-slider DIC rating validation.

**Nuanced findings and explicit methodological gaps:** Within THIS product set roast differences were largest, then brew temperature, with late immersion time smaller as TDS plateaued. Perceived sweetness was negatively associated with TDS, 19 other attributes positively correlated; these are conditional associations, NOT proof that reducing espresso yield/extraction/temperature increases member sweetness. Authors acknowledge mixing dilution, chilled service and overnight storage could alter effects; a light-roast short-hot vs long-cold multivariate similarity does not prove equivalence and needs discrimination testing. Original Methods says **27** selected attributes while Results says **28**, unresolved without the supplement. Funded by SCA, with Toddy underwriting.
