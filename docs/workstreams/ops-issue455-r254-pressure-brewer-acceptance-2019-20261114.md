1. **R455-EV-CIT-254-A — sensory method.** Publisher §2.4 and §3.7, Fig. 7 caption: **three expert tasters, one rating per taster**, categories fragrance, aroma, acidity, bitterness, body, aftertaste and overall impression; Fig. 7 reports group-average n=3. Staresso had highest overall impression and the Espresso method displayed marked bitterness *under those preparation conditions*. **Grade:** direct author method/results, very limited three-person tasting, no blinded consumer preference generalization, five individual DIC inputs, same-coffee setup/style/grinder repeated-shot intervention or personal recommendation efficacy. Minor paper wording calls these “gravity filtration” in §2.4 despite the article's five pressure methods; avoid correcting the authors silently. **App:** no change to five ratings or confidence claims.
2. **R455-EV-CIT-254-B — comparability and confounding.** Publisher §§2.1–2.3: one farm/variety (Castillo, Anserma), one controlled medium roast; five machines *simultaneously varied* pressure method, water volume, grind and contact time. Espresso used **14 g ground coffee, 75 mL input hot water, ~90 °C, fine nominal 400 microns**, while Aeropress used 14 g, 150 mL water, 90 °C, four minutes, medium nominal 526 microns. **75 mL is input WATER, not recorded espresso beverage YIELD and cannot be translated into a DIC 1:5.36 measured yield recipe**. Pressure 5–18 bar described as a broad machine-dependent reference, **not a measured per-shot instrument trace**. **Grade:** original experimental recipe report with uncontrolled cross-method differences; no single-variable within-espresso causal recipe inference or Turbo vs Fast-Flow transfer.
3. **R455-EV-CIT-254-C — chemical versus sensory outcomes.** Publisher §§3.2–3.7: different preparations' phenolics and antioxidant assay measurements differ; ORAC and ABTS rankings are **not identical** (e.g., ABTS ranks Presso above Staresso, ORAC ranks Staresso above Presso). Panel flavor/profile ratings and lab antioxidant assays are **different outcomes**. The paper's own “recommend” language on antioxidant retention is not proof of improved consumer espresso taste, nutrition/clinical outcomes, or an app-specific grind, yield, temperature, pressure or sensory success numeric policy. **Grade:** published experimental assay/three-expert panel, reported n=3; figures/raw data not independently reanalyzed.
