## Exact bounded primary-study claims, pages and evidence grades

1. **R445-XCT-A, printed pp. 5–7, methods §§3.1–3.3; mechanism supported in two sample coffees.** Two Square Mile roasted coffees, Tumba Rwanda and Guayacán Colombia, were ground at **11 settings each** with one Mahlkönig grinder (22 powder samples), sized dry with Camsizer X2, mounted in **5 mm straws** and imaged by XCT at **2.69/2.99 µm voxels**. Critically the authors explicitly **did NOT tamp or control sample packing/porosity** for scanning. The method validates a relationship conditional on measured porosity and grain characteristics, **not an actual barista-tamped espresso puck**.
2. **R445-XCT-B, printed pp. 7–8, §§3.4–3.5; model-to-model agreement, not measured beverage pressure/flow.** Permeability was estimated through **lattice-Boltzmann flow simulations on segmented XCT pore domains**, using **air** parameters, an imposed numerically tiny pressure gradient and low-Re creeping-flow assumptions. Authors state preliminary direct bench permeability measurements were impractical with screens. Their empirical comparison is therefore XCT-derived simulated permeability versus analytic model, **not an independent wet espresso machine/basket flow, extracted beverage, or blinded sensory result**. A fluid-independent geometrical permeability assumption does not independently validate actual wet puck swelling/fines migration.
3. **R445-XCT-C, printed pp. 9–13, table 1, figures 5–7 and §§4–5; conditional useful mechanism.** Total/connected porosity, interfacial specific surface and particle sizes inform computed permeabilities. Percolation-derived geometry aligns with the authors' computed results for tested coffees and settings; uncertainty and sensitivity to packing remain relevant. Table 1 contains one explicit **Guayacán setting 2 'no data'** XCT/permeability row: 22 ground samples is **not** 22 completed permeability measurements; do not describe full results as an unqualified 22/22 replicated dataset. The original numbers should not be carried into grinder-relative dial scales without machine-specific PSD/porosity calibration.
4. **R445-XCT-D, printed pp. 15–17, §§5.3–6; extrapolation, not an approved espresso recipe.** The example `38–42g in 27–32s at 92 C`, `0.3–0.5` assumed connected porosity and estimated Forchheimer values are **illustrative modeled espresso assumptions**, not measured human preferences or universal numeric optima. The authors explicitly call for future dynamic-flow work, and identify contested water-swelling/fines migration effects. Their later text prints a likely **unit inconsistency** `90,000–100,000 Pa (9–10 bar)` (9–10 bar is 900,000–1,000,000 Pa), so do not reuse this as verified pressure calibration. A second printed swelling paragraph appears to drop a negative exponent while describing a permeability decrease; treat it as **unresolved editorial/numerical ambiguity**, not an operational correction.
5. **R445-XCT-E, effectiveness grade UNESTABLISHED.** No study participants taste the coffee, no all-five DIC ratings, same-coffee next-shot intervention, consented improvement measurement, different espresso grinder calibrations, alternative Turbo/Fast-Flow controlled comparisons, or current production app experiment. Stronger mechanistic primary-source provenance does **not** upgrade subjective cup-quality confidence or numerical policy authority.
