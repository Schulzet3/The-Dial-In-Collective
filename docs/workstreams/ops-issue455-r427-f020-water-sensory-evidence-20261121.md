## Specific original F020 claim and independent conditions

- **F020 statement:** `Water alkalinity affects perceived acidity and acid buffering, but hardness alone does not specify espresso outcome.` Original classification `well_supported_derived_inference`, internal grade **B**, no established numeric effect, `actionable_with_current_app_inputs=false`. - SCA article is an original technical explanation by Marco Wellinger, **not** an independently controlled five-taste DIC outcome trial. It explains buffer alkalinity vs pH and beverage-ratio dependence, explicitly flags unknown effects of hardness and **scale/corrosion** constraints: a naive multiplication of filter-water targets is **not** safe espresso-machine advice. Its illustrative ranges are not DIC numeric rules.
- Rune et al. studied **one** medium-roast Colombian Kachalu coffee via **French press**: 50 g coffee / ~900 g brew water, 92°C pouring temperature, four-minute brew. Two untrained discriminatory sessions (31 and 25 participants) and two descriptive panels (10 trained assessors) tested controlled mineral additions; descriptive ratings used **15-cm intensity lines, not DIC's five independent 1–5 member taste ratings**.
- Duo-trio higher **bicarbonate** 120 mg/L (p=0.049) and 240 mg/L (p<0.001) distinguished from ultrapure water; 15–60 mg/L bicarbonate comparisons did **not** reach p<0.05. Magnesium-only series did not discriminate; isolated calcium result at 15 mg/L (p=0.022) lacked dose consistency. In high-mineral descriptive comparisons, 300 mg/L bicarbonate lowered acidic/fruity/complexity and raised bitter/roasted perceptions; calcium-only had no reliable sensory difference. Source tables/units distinguish added HCO3 mg/L from alkalinity expressed **as CaCO3** — do not substitute units.
- The **actual paper** acknowledges one sensory coffee/roast, **no measured TDS or extraction yield**, no organic acid speciation, and further pH-only tests on three other origins do not establish equivalent **sensory** response. Calcium carbonate precipitation altered effective bicarbonate in combined waters. These limitations prohibit extrapolating thresholds, optimal water recipes, hardness preferences or sensory effect sizes to espresso, all 7 roasts, Turbo/Fast-Flow or any person's machine.
