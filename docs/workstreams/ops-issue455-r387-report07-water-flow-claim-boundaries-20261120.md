**R387-07-MINERAL-TRANSFER (B for specific filtered-coffee chemistry, D for direct espresso/taste transfer; no product rule):** Owner #07 lines 39, 195, 229, 306, 371 and bibliography line 392 cite Bratthäll, Figueira & Nording, *Heliyon* (2024), DOI [10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e26625](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e26625), original full author paper [PMC10907646](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10907646). Direct methods, not merely a news headline: **one dark-roasted Arabica blend**, ground **coarse for French press**, **11 g** coffee steeped in **200 mL** at **93°C** for **5 minutes**, vacuum-filtered. Two reagent-grade salts **MgCl2 and CaCl2** added at **100 or 1,000 ppm** either *before* or *after* brewing; five replicates per group; GC-MS/NMR measured selected **organic acids** (citric, malic, lactic, quinic), **not** complete EY/TDS, espresso flow or member sensory ratings. At ordinary test concentrations, acids varied little; at 10-fold, larger changes. Pre/post differences were small in most comparisons. This supports a **conditional negative result for measured acids in a specific immersion/filter protocol**, not "all minerals have no extraction effect" or a quantified espresso mineral adjustment; authors' sensory interpretation is hypothetical, no blind five-taste trial. Owner #07 is directionally cautious but its broad 'keep predicted bulk EY small' design suggestion would be untested if translated into app coefficient. **Status:** source method/abstract independently inspected, specific chemical claim VERIFIED_WITH_BOUNDED_SCOPE; espresso recommendation extrapolation NOT_VERIFIED/HELD.

**R387-07-FLOW-CONDITIONALITY (B for studied one-coffee espresso chemistry, D for taste/other equipment extrapolation):** Owner #07 lines 15, 33, 225, 269 and bibliography line 387 cite Schmieder, Pannusch et al., *Foods* (2023), DOI [10.3390/foods12152871](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods12152871), original journal [article](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/12/15/2871), first-party [TUM record](https://www.lse.ls.tum.de/en/btd/news/article/new-publication-on-influence-of-flow-rate-particle-size-and-temperature-on-espresso-extraction-kinetics/), archived author [institutional study PDF](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/download/1720325/1720325.pdf). Reported controlled design with **one washed Colombia Suprema Huila Arabica**, two roast batches cross-checked, **20-g dose**, successive fractions analyzed for caffeine, trigonelline, 5-CQA and TDS. The article's conclusion is that programmed flow was the strongest of **tested three process variables** on measured cup components, **but beverage mass/brew ratio changes were larger**; example at central point 1 vs 2 mL/s only shifted 5-CQA concentrations 3.11 vs 2.99 mg/g at 1:2, comparable to modest output-mass variation. Flow sensitivity depended on grind and temperature. **This does not establish an empirically optimal temperature, flow, shot time or sensory improvement for DIC.** Current controlled test did not measure all five DIC taste ratings or next-shot causal outcomes; machine and coffee-specific scope. **Status:** paper citation/authors, abstract, comparative methods/conclusions independently checked from publisher-indexed text and first-party university source; primary full paper figures/table statistical reanalysis NOT performed.
