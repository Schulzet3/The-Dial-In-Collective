**Physical measurements, not taste validation:** Researchers used commercial/in-house roasted coffee, an aligned Mahlkönig EK43 with calibrated burr chirp, Faraday-cup charge-to-mass, size analysis and moisture/color instruments; several conditions were measured in triplicate. Wetting before grinding reduced net charge and coarse/fine aggregates under tested conditions, with charge tending toward zero near **20 microliters of water per gram** of whole beans; grind, roast and residual moisture modified responses. In one illustrated espresso condition the paper held **18 g dry grounds / 45 g beverage, 94°C, 7-bar static water pressure, 196 N tamp, 2 s preinfusion**, Victoria Arduino Black Eagle, fixed grinder setting. Wetting produced ~**50% longer** shot time and **8.9% versus 8.2% beverage TDS**; the Fig.7 flow fits use an **average of three wet and three dry espresso shots**. These particular TDSs imply approximately **8.5% relative concentration difference** (0.7 percentage points), **not** a universal increase in extracted solubles or perceived quality. Source interprets these as conditional charge/aggregate/bed-permeability effects and *hypothesizes* flavor implications; it did **not measure DIC's five taste ratings, blind preferences or any universal optimal pre-grind water dose**. Its later [2024 iScience mitigation paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2024.110639) is a **different experiment**; do not transfer its darker-roast >=15% concentration observation to this Matter study.
