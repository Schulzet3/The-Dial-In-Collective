## Original study — claim-level source review
Gunning, Serantes Laforgue, Guinard and Tagkopoulos, *AI-driven prediction of consumer liking of coffee from sensory data*, npj Science of Food 10:142, published March 14 2026, DOI [10.1038/s41538-026-00779-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41538-026-00779-7). Directly read original open publisher HTML Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods and data/code links on 2026-11-12. Dataset is **reanalysis**, not another sensory experiment: Cotter et al.'s one medium-roast coffee as **27 black DRIP brews** spanning 3 extraction yields × 3 brew strengths/TDS × 3 brewing temperatures, each rated by **118 California consumers** (3,186 person×brew rows). Ratings were 9-point hedonic overall liking plus *just-about-right adequacy* (acidity, flavor intensity, mouthfeel) and **17 check-all-that-apply descriptors**, NOT DIC's five distinct 1–5 espresso ratings.

Published nonaggregated 80/20 row-level holdout: 3 sensory-feature classifier 0.75 accuracy / 0.81 AUROC, regression R² 0.47 / 9-point MAE ~1.03. This holdout is **not** a demonstrated disjoint test of new drinkers or entirely unseen coffees: 27 brews each recur across consumer rows, while original article does not demonstrate that extrapolation. For 27 brew-level aggregated samples, the authors instead used nested leave-one-out CV with **no separate holdout**. Preference clustering gave 57 versus 61 consumers with divergent chocolate/roast versus fruit/floral tendencies. The dataset and authors' Discussion limit extension to another coffee, roast, consumer population or espresso. Reported liking accuracy and driver correlation do **not** identify the causal effect of raising espresso yield or changing grind or validate an app-wide '75% confidence' indicator.

Evidence: [original publisher Methods/Results/Discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41538-026-00779-7), listed [Dryad data](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.25338/B8993H), listed [authors' code](https://github.com/mhgun/sensory_prediction).
