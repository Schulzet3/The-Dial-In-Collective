## Primary methods, bounded findings, contradiction, limitations
- Eighteen retailer-labelled coffees spanning Arabica/Robusta/blends, three commercial roast labels and origin labels; each sample subjected to **three technical chromatographic analyses**, not three independent consumer sensory trials. Four brewing *packages*, not randomized one-variable espresso modifications: **espresso 7g ground coffee and 25mL input water, 25–30 seconds at 90–96C**; simple immersion/French Press/V60 **15g and 250mL water** with different grind/contact/temperature. No reported factorial sweep of espresso yield, grind or five sensory ratings; no machine-specific calibrated grinder settings or dose/yield **output mass**. Do NOT translate 7g/25mL *water input* into a DIC beverage yield-by-weight ratio.
- HPLC caffeine, Folin–Ciocalteu **total** phenolics, and ICP–OES minerals measured. Results §§2.1 and Discussion: espresso tended to have higher **concentrations** of caffeine, TPC, K, Mg, P than compared preparations; V60 tended higher Ca/Fe/Na. Results §2.5 and Discussion explicitly clarify that the much smaller **25mL espresso serving** contains less total caffeine than a **250mL other-method serving** in this study. Concentration (per 100mL) and quantity per serving are different denominators; neither predicts a personalized sensory slider or optimal shot. Note §2.4 sentence calling espresso lowest among analyzed minerals appears in tension with §2.1 and Discussion about high K/Mg/P concentrations; **do not resolve without raw Tables/units**.
- Roast-label PCA p=0.784 and origin-label PCA p=0.862 for the **measured chemical endpoints** do not show that roast or origin do not affect flavor or that species alone controls espresso sensory outcomes. Retailer classifications and Folin assay cross-reactivity (reducing sugars etc.) are acknowledged limitations. Study has no human sensory participant panel, no experimental next-shot correction, no privacy/Community validation. Do NOT infer health, caffeine intake for a specific member, uniform grinder compensation, a new five-taste weighting or fixed pressure/temperature/time optimum.
