## Distinct original source identity, access and methods

Source ID proposal: LIT-2026-LO-VECCHIO-ESPRESSO-MOKA-KAMIRA. Original publisher full HTML: https://www.mdpi.com/2297-8739/13/6/166 (Introduction, Methods, statistics, Results/Tables 2, 4 and 5, Discussion, Conclusions, conflict and data-access sections directly read). Publisher lists a 22-page original. Accessible and substantively reviewed at 2026-11-13. An immutable source-PDF SHA-256, raw HTML byte hash, supplementary figures and author-request raw observations were NOT obtained; publisher URL/DOI and publication version are the traceable identity, not a fabricated binary verification.

One retail Arabica/Robusta blend (Lavazza Crema e Gusto) and one bottled water were used. The nominal device recipes were DIFFERENT: home Dedica espresso about 7 g grounds → 25 mL in about 10 s (pump nominal 15 bar, not demonstrated puck pressure), moka about 12 g and Kamira about 8 g. Differences cannot be attributed to machine alone because dose, output, pressure, profile, filtration and contact time were not experimentally matched. The team pooled multiple preparations to approximately 400 mL per method for chemical analyses, and performed analytical triplicates on pooled material. Those repetitions DO NOT prove n=3 independent brew/puck outcomes.

Ten trained/familiarized assessors rated six coded samples (duplicate presentations of three brewing methods) in one randomized session. Duplicate ratings were median-aggregated within assessor/method, with Friedman repeated-measures tests and Holm-adjusted paired Wilcoxon post hoc comparisons. This is a small method-comparison panel, NOT a personalized, longitudinal, same-coffee, same-grinder, one-variable next-shot study.

## Atomic claim audit

**R455-EV-CIT-232-A — CHEMICAL METHOD COMPARISON, directly source-supported, transfer LOW.** Publisher Table 4 reports for its one recipe/method espresso caffeine 55.3 ± 4.1 mg per 100 g beverage, and Table 2 total solids 2.61 ± 0.11 g/100 g. These are sample-specific measured outputs with propagated/analytical uncertainty, not universal espresso intensity, caffeine, target yield, TDS, extraction-time or personal preference norms. The design confounds hardware with different brew recipes, and pooled assay replication is not independent brew variation. Evidence: primary full text sections 2.1.1, 2.5.3, Tables 2 and 4. Rating: primary-method observation HIGH confidence in authors' report; DIC actionable generalization INSUFFICIENT.

**R455-EV-CIT-232-B — SENSORY METHOD COMPARISON, directly source-supported, causal efficacy NOT verified.** Publisher Table 5 gives cross-method global p=0.032258 for sweetness and p=0.011615 for harmony (specific significant pairwise comparisons chiefly involve moka); acidity p=0.973684, bitterness p=0.696902, body p=0.214381 and aftertaste p=0.442053 were not significantly different in the small panel. No statistically significant espresso-vs-Kamira post hoc pairwise results are established by that table. Six coded samples are two ratings per method, not six independent coffees or independent users. A panel's subjective “repeatability” sensory descriptor does NOT measure repeat espresso-machine extraction consistency across shots. Evidence: primary sections 2.6.1–2.6.3, Table 5 and Discussion. Rating: reported within-panel descriptive comparison HIGH; user-specific preference, replication and next-shot improvement UNVERIFIED.

No claim that non-significance proves equivalence. No unreported effect sizes, confidence percentages, additional coffee identities, member outcomes, commercial benefits or causality inferred. Raw data available only on author request per publication. External replication not assessed.
