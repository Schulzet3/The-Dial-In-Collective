## Two claim-level source checks
1. **R455-EV-CIT-170-A, direct controlled recipe description (high confidence in reported methods; low transfer to DIC recipes).** Authors used five commercial Arabica coffees and four brew *methods*, but grind and roast were selected per method. Espresso used a KRUPS Calvi XP3440, 7 g dose / 25 mL beverage, 15 bar, 90°C and 30 s; other methods varied dose, brew time, water temperature and filters. Coffee rested four days, EK43 was used at method-dependent grind. These are protocol values, not generalized optimum prescriptions; original study did not randomize isolated grind, roast, flow, ratio or temperature while holding the others constant. Original §§2.1,2.3,2.5 and Table 2; publisher lines 200–240.
2. **R455-EV-CIT-170-B, bounded sensory/chemistry result and internal consistency warning.** Seven panelists aged 20–40 assessed SCA-style categories across changing serving temperatures; **not** the five DIC self-rating scales. Original §§2.10–2.11 and §3.5 report higher overall preference among this panel for selected espresso and cold brew methods, with Colombia cold brew 91.86±1.82 and exceptions depending on coffee. Antioxidant assays, HPLC concentrations and GC-MS peak areas do not calibrate perceived sweetness, personal success or health benefits. The paper's *abstract* and Results §3.1 use **86.31 ± 0.70 μmol/100 mL** for Colombia espresso TEAC, but Conclusion §4 says **86.31 ± 0.70 mmol/100 mL**, a three-orders-of-magnitude unit discrepancy; do not reuse the number or assume which unit the authors intended without source confirmation. Original lines 175–179, 285–295, 299–305, 350–369. Raw data and figure pixels were not independently validated.
