**Experimental scope:** Three Arabica bean samples (two same-origin Yellow Bourbon batches at different roast levels), four grinders: Mahlkönig EK43, Bosch TSM6A013B impact-knife, Comandante C40, C60; 30 bean/grinder/setting samples planned, laser diffraction HELOS R6 9–1750 µm with **three PSD replicates per sample**. Two coarsest C40/light/dark samples exceeded the same lens's range, so **28** entered the ten five-parameter two-distribution fit comparison: **22** distinctly bimodal, **six** weakly bimodal. Three combinations **Lognormal–Weibull (LW), Weibull–Betaprime (WB), Gamma–Weibull (GW)** fit clear bimodality comparably; no single winner for weak bimodality. Reported average fit errors across 28: GW 0.787 ± 0.315, WB 0.797 ± 0.317, LW 0.815 ± 0.421 mm⁻¹; these are **model PSD-fit errors**, not cup flavor scores or espresso uncertainty intervals. Grinder/burr versus impact-knife and grind setting changed observed PSD/model fit; modest conditional roast effects with limited beans, not a universal lack of effect. Publisher notes supportive public funding, no declared conflicts, and authors' use of ChatGPT/Grammarly for manuscript language with author responsibility.

**Limit:** No brewing/espresso extraction, taste panel, DIC five-dimensional ratings, slow-feed comparison, grinder calibration transfer, pressure/temperature control, actual shot time, cross-coffee trials, or causal recipe improvement was tested. Bin-averaged PSD regression on 28 selected samples does not identify optimal member grind number, a universal fines proportion, preferred espresso style, sensory target, or confidence probability. Two out-of-lens excluded samples and higher fit error for weakly bimodal ground samples limit transfer. This paper supports only **research-level caution** about grinder-/setting-dependent particle populations and the inadequacy of single-diameter simplifications for some distributions, not new app rules.
