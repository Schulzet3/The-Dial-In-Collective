## Three bounded independent source claims, with strength and uncertainty
- `R455-EV-291-A` (method, publisher identity HIGH / transcription detail MODERATE): Experimental §2.2 used 7.5 g coffee, 25 mL beverage, 25 s extraction, with pump-machine A at a *combined nine-condition* 3x3 grid of 7/9/11 bar and 88/92/98 °C. Grind was selected to achieve the fixed recipe, so this was not a controlled one-variable next-shot DIC feedback test. A scoped finding for this configuration, **not** a universal best recipe or 25 mL target. Accepted transcription lines 153–176.
- `R455-EV-291-B` (analytical evidence MODERATE, external application LOW): §2.4–2.5/§3.2 measured relative HS-SPME-GC/MS peak areas, using triplicate chemistry measurements and normalization to the lowest-condition signal. Authors explicitly called the odorant abundances **semiquantitative**, not absolute concentration ratios. §3.2 reports a favorable 92 °C/9 bar condition *on that test apparatus and samples*, alongside similar panel trends; it is not sufficient to assert an individual universal optimal temperature/pressure, DIC sensory confidence or improved cup quality. Accepted transcription lines 188–226, 256–299 and Fig. 2–3 captions.
- `R455-EV-291-C` (human-sensory method MODERATE, DIC external validity LOW): §2.6 used eight trained IIAC panel judges, training across two 8-hour sessions, repeated assessment in isolated randomized serving conditions; §3.2 reported 0–10 global positive/negative olfactive indices and averaged panel scores, **NOT** the app's five personal 1–5 taste ratings. §3.1 showed pump A's near-constant 9 bar / ~93 °C versus lever B's ~8 to 2 bar and ~101 °C declining ~10 °C, warning against treating nominal machine setting as a portable identical extraction profile. The article reports Nuova Simonelli supplied samples/machines and partial financial support. No prospective same-coffee, same-grinder, single-change -> next-shot five-taste outcome was measured. Accepted transcription lines 227–255, 280–299, 383–387; Fig. 1 caption.
