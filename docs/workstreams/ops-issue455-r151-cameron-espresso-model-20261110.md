## Primary study directly investigated
- Cameron, Morisco, Hofstetter et al., [Systematically Improving Espresso: Insights from Mathematical Modeling and Experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matt.2019.12.019), *Matter* 2(3), 631–648, published March 4, 2020 (author institution also lists online January 22, 2020). Read source-indexed original paper's Introduction, mathematical homogeneous-bed model, ratio/pressure model, Brewing Espresso experimental method/results, Figure 4 interpretation, two optimization alternatives, cafe implementation and Outlook. Crosschecked identity with McMaster and University of Huddersfield research records. Direct publisher DOI open 403; **not** PDF-binary verified, complete supplemental/figure pixel/raw experiment reanalysis or independently reproduced.
- Model assumes homogeneous liquid flow and predicts monotonic EY improvement from finer grind. Actual 6-bar pressure cafe test showed a **peak** in EY vs grind, with low EY at very fine settings; the authors lowered pressure from 9 bar due to clogging in fine settings. Tamping standardized at 98 N with no appreciable EY/time effect in *those* trials; flavor differences noted but **not quantified**. Inference of local inhomogeneous flow is plausible model/experiment comparison, not proof every sour/bitter shot has the same physical cause.
- 20 g→15 g/coarser-grind strategy and ~14 s extraction were used in a specific cafe, with illustrative savings for 27,850 drinks. EY/cafe revenue ≠ validated preferred five-taste espresso. Authors state EY is not directly a beverage-quality measure; no blind home users, five DIC ratings, cross-machine grinder tuning, style-specific recipe success or app recommendation field trial. Do **not** encode one universal optimal grind, dose, 14 s, 6 bar, maximum EY or flavor certainty.
