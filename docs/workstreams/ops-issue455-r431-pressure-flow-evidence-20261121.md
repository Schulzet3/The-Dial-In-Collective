## Claim-by-claim direct primary/manufacturer checks

1. **Machine control available (manufacturer-direct, grade A *for described product capability*, not consumer performance).** La Marzocco's official [Strada EP feature page](https://www.lamarzoccousa.com/commercial-products/espresso-machines/strada-ep/) documents electronic-paddle gear pumps, direct pressure control and four programmable pressure profiles per group; records and replays pressure curves. This substantiates *some dedicated equipment can execute programmed pressure profiles*, **not** that every owner machine can, nor that any fixed pressure curve necessarily yields better espresso. Its tasting adjectives are manufacturer marketing, not independently controlled outcome trials.
2. **Pressure/flow priority implementation (manufacturer-direct, grade A for interface/feature only).** The official [Decent DE1 manual](https://decentespresso.com/docs/de1_manual) (search-indexed manual passage, direct full manual fetch unavailable in this run) distinguishes basic Pressure/Flow editors and advanced step/limit controls, plus explicit dispensed liquid volume vs scale-measured mass. [Decent product overview](https://www.decentespresso.com/overview) explicitly advertises flow-vs-pressure priority and graphs. The [Decent pressure-vs-flow explanation](https://decentespresso.com/docs/pressure_vs_flow_profiling_and_how_to_convert_into_an_advanced_shot) was **search-index accessible only; full page retrieval failed**, so the detailed prose about flow caps and pressure decline is *partially corroborated by first-party excerpts*, not a full-document peer review. Its recommendations that certain curves consistently taste best are *vendor opinion, not causal validation*, and must NOT become DIC rules.
3. **Physical coupling (primary experimental/model source, grade B for studied hydraulic phenomenon; extrapolation grade C/UNVERIFIED).** Waszkiewicz et al., *Under pressure: poroelastic regulation of flow in espresso brewing*, full author [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21528v2) (2026-05-09), Sections I–II, IV–V, reports non-linear pressure–flow behavior and compaction/dissolution coupling. Critically, Sections II.1–II.2 used **one Brazilian light/medium-light coffee, 18.50 ± 0.05 g fixed dose, fixed grinder setting 1.9, one Sanremo Zoe/IMS basket, and ~120-s brews to study 60+s long-time hydraulics**. Sections I, V expressly characterize the constitutive model as coarse-grained/phenomenological; limitations include no independent wetted-coffee constitutive tests. The paper is **not** randomized tasting evidence for 6/9/12 bar, does not test DIC's 5 ratings, machine presets or all five styles, and cannot calibrate a universally optimal pressure or flow.
4. **“Pauses cause channeling” is conditional, not a new rule.** That paper's Section IV Fig. 10 stop/restart observation involved **~75s brewing at 8 bar followed by repeated on/off cycles with a backflowing three-way valve**; Fig. 10 interpretation explicitly lacked imaging of internal reconfiguration. This specific condition cannot be generalized into a categorical ban on a blooming espresso or controlled no-backflow soak, nor proof that all pauses ruin cups. Treat as mechanistic *risk hypothesis* only; no new user-facing correction.
5. **No independent sensory effectiveness.** No primary comparative human five-rating next-shot trial, within-coffee/setup/profile single-variable counterfactual, objective machine curve logs or DIC outcome instrumentation was reviewed here. Assertions about consistent sweetness, body, channeling, improved quality, grinder notch counts, universal 3/6/9/12-bar targets are **NOT VERIFIED**. Do not translate them into numeric app policies or a “scientifically confident” recommendation.
