## Bounded direct source findings

1. **Specific experimental context (printed pp. 2322–2323).** One medium-dark supplied coffee, **7.0 g** ground dose, Vibiemme V220, **92 °C**, **9 atm**, 1,500 g tamp-force setting, three *Mazzer Super Jolly grinder-specific* dial settings **6, 6.5, 7** (fine to coarser). The authors separated brews into **0–8 s, 9–16 s, 17–24 s** fractions, plus nominal **25 mL** regular cups, and describe at least 20 replicates per espresso / 240 samples. Different experiments/settings cannot be silently generalized to a modern 18 g basket or another calibrated grinder.
2. **Crucial measured-volume confound (Table 3, printed p. 2323 and discussion).** Mean first-8-s fraction volumes for 6/6.5/7 are **16.239 ± 3.579**, **28.858 ± 2.318**, **41.847 ± 1.701 mL** respectively; reported mean percolation rates across settings **1.66 ± 0.19**, **2.89 ± 0.59**, **6.51 ± 0.26 mL/s**. Thus a fixed eight-second interval collected dramatically different brew volumes, and the two coarser settings already exceeded the nominal 25 mL “regular coffee” volume. The paper's first-fraction acid/solids/caffeine concentration findings **must not** become a universal eight-second extraction cutoff, ratio, sensory target or one-control next-shot correction. Units are **volume** not current member-recorded **yield mass**.
3. **Instrument output, not a consumer taste study (printed pp. 2322–2326).** The “electronic nose” was six metal-oxide semiconductor sensors run on headspace of coffee aliquots; it recorded electrical responses. Within this experimental sample set, the paper reports PCA **PC1=98.77% / PC2=1.01%** for combined fractions/grinds, and distinguishes sampled aroma-response profiles. Those PCA variance fractions are **not** a held-out classifier score, calibrated confidence of DIC personal five-taste predictions, blinded human liking, or an observed improvement in next shots. Table 4 sensor maxima support only the within-study instrumental contrast.
