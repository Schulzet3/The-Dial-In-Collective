## Three source-bounded claims
1. **Preparation / participants (Methods 2.1–2.3).** One 72-hour fermented Topázio arabica from Brazil roasted to light/medium/dark in respective 10/11/12 min curves, **Bunn G3 autodrip grind and Bunn VP17 electric drip brewing at 1g:16mL**; *not* pressurized espresso, not multiple coffees or grinder-step comparison. Sixteen trained panelists (three Q-graders) and 63 self-selected weekly specialty consumers (university/café snowball sample) assessed three roasts, with medium duplicated (M1/M2), using a predefined eight-option categorical response set.
2. **What was measured (Results 3/3.1).** In the study's 50-second Temporal Dominance of Sensations test, trained tasters more often recorded sour/sweet/fruity in lighter samples; the consumer group often selected bitter/fermented/no-perception, and both groups commonly marked dark roast as bitter. **Dominance** means percentage choosing a descriptor at a time, **not** liking, intensity, DIC's five independent 1–5 member ratings, predicted shot quality, model certainty or proof an espresso change worked. Their 12.5% chance and 20% significance lines describe eight-option source analysis, never universal taste thresholds.
3. **Limits / unresolved discrepancy.** Methods 2.1 says finished coffee maintained at **65°C**; Methods 2.3 says 10-mL tasting samples **served at 80°C**. Original text doesn't independently reconcile these numbers; no guessed correction. Forced 50s TDS with explicit 'No Perception' button **is not a blank/missing DIC taste rating**. The duplicated medium presentation is not a controlled yield/grind recommendation → next-shot result. Authors note 50-second duration may truncate persistent sensations and duplicate 'bitter' descriptor can shift descriptor competition (Discussion 4). Raw individual consumer responses not independently reanalyzed.
