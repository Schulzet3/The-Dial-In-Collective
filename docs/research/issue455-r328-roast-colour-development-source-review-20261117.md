## Methods and bounded primary facts

- Eight roast-process studies: studies 1–6 included trained panelists (7–11 per study); studies 7/8 used respectively 49 and 46 briefly calibrated coffee professionals. Studied **washed** Kenya, Colombia, Ethiopia coffees, largely Probat roaster, Study 6 Loring. Study 1 prior-published wider roast defects, other seven narrower contrasts. Each condition tasted in three replicates (four in studies 5/6), blinded/randomized (Methods 2.1–2.3).
- Critically, these were **not espresso**: **50 g coarsely ground coffee plus 900 mL 92°C water in French press**, 3 minutes 30 seconds, served at 55°C to expert panels; attribute intensities on a **15 cm absent–a lot** scale (PDF p.7 / Methods 2.3). None is an application's personal five 1–5 rating or a paired next-shot tasting outcome.
- Direct **Table 4** comparison of the **only two studies** varying both roast colour and timing: average absolute correlation for colour vs development-time **0.40 vs 0.21** in Study 1, **0.46 vs 0.07** in Study 2. Study 1 darker-colour signed direction (table colour already sign-reversed): bitterness **+0.66**, sweetness **−0.34**, fruitiness **−0.57**; Study 2 bitterness **+0.59**, sweetness **−0.44**, fruitiness **−0.49**. These are **within-study associations and descriptive panel attributes**, not causal individualized espresso targets (PDF pp.8–9, Table 4).
- In the remaining seven-study regression, development phase of roasting mattered more than time-to-first-crack under tested conditions, but original authors warn Table 5 **body/sweetness slopes very close to zero**, sometimes statistically significant due sample size rather than practical sensory effect; do not translate into an app numeric target or 'science confidence'. Paper explicitly limits inference to its roast range (Agtron 46–99), washed beans, roaster/brew types and limited assessor vocabulary (PDF pp.10–12).
