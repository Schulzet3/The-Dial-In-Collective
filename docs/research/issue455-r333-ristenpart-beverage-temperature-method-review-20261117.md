## Audited findings (all require context; no app defaults)

1. **R455-EV-R333-TASTING-VERSUS-BREW** (Methods > Definitions; Study overview; Coffee service): A *commercial drip-brew* factorial used initial brew-water 87/90/93 °C and controlled beverage strength and extraction yield. These are **brewer input** temperatures, not tasting temperatures. Roughly 30 mL black coffee in a 120 mL paper cup was held for a 90 s instructed wait. Mean *initial tasting* temperature was 64.1 °C, SD 2.4 °C, range 56–71 °C. A research assistant, *not each participant individually*, recorded representative post-pour and 90-second temperatures across 162 brews. Evidence: original publisher study Methods and Fig. 1 caption. **Grade:** direct peer-reviewed methods/report; no independently reprocessed raw observations. Distinct from the DIC's controlled espresso machine extraction temperature input `tempC`.
2. **R455-EV-R333-REPEATED-UNCONTROLLED** (Methods > Data and statistical analysis; Results Figs. 2–3, Table 1): 118 people each assessed 27 coffee conditions (3,186 total stated tastings). *Tasting temperature was not separately assigned/randomized*; it arose from process variation. Some people supplied repeated assessments over temperatures, and temperature-bin sample sizes were unequal. Authors accordingly use a repeated-participant hottest/mean/coldest comparison and Stuart–Maxwell analysis for paired JAR response distributions, not an independent-group claim. The original linked 2023 Dryad dataset metadata describes 3,168 rows (versus 3,186 assessments expected); exact 18-record discrepancy remains unresolved without original file; do not treat counts as independently reconciled. **Grade:** source text independently reviewed; design/causal limits material; no original participant CSV inspection.
3. **R455-EV-R333-JAR-NOT-ESPRESSO-TASTE** (Consumer test; Results Figs. 2–6; Discussion): Tested *tasting-temperature adequacy* using a five-point **just-about-right** scale, overall coffee liking on a **nine-point hedonic scale**, flavor intensity/acidity/mouthfeel adequacy on distinct five-point JAR questions, CATA and purchase intent. Temperature-adequacy JAR distributions changed across hottest/coldest, while differences in liking and other sensory adequacy were smaller. Authors discuss 58–66 °C for consumer acceptance and 68–70 °C to minimize 'too cold' responses within tested 56–71 °C. These are **observed starting-drinking temperatures in commercial drip black coffee**, not safe/optimal espresso brew temperatures, not five DIC extraction/body/clarity/sweetness/astringency ratings, and not within-member single-change next-shot improvements. The hottest range had sparse observations. **Grade:** peer-reviewed reported group associations; nonrandom temperature and multiple repeated observations prevent causal individual espresso inference.
