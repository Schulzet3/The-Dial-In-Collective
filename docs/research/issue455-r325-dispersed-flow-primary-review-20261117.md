## R455-EV-PRIMARY-325-A — Tracer pulse and actual dynamics

Methods 2.2–2.4 and Results 3.1–3.2 / original Tables 1–3 (original print pp.3,6–7): coffee was brewed under earlier hot ~88 ± 2°C conditions, then its bed transferred to an **independent** ~22°C KCl tracer-pulse test. Four particle-size distributions were examined; Distribution B was repeated at initial/dry, 30g, 60g previously extracted brew stages; each of the plotted curves was a triplicate. Table 3 Bodenstein number Bo (mean ± indicated variation) **16.84 ± 0.47 (initial), 10.01 ± 2.82 (after 30g), 7.74 ± 1.29 (after 60g)**. Within the tested flow configuration, Bo decreases as axial dispersion increases over extraction stage. Important: these numerical points are NOT universal home espresso seconds, extraction ratio, puck channeling measurement, or a verified cause (fines migration/swelling/erosion/consolidation remain possible).

## R455-EV-PRIMARY-325-B — Model fit, validation, and boundaries

Methods 2.5–2.7 and Results 3.3–3.6 / Appendix 4.5 (print pp.4,7–12): fitted concentration kinetics for PSD A report **TDS R²=.99**, three measured analyte concentration curves average **R²=.83**. The fitted-parameter model used to predict related PSD B and C reports **TDS R²=.95/.97**, component averages **.90/.95**. The modeled analytes are caffeine, trigonelline and 5-CQA; no human taste panel. Important assumptions: prescribed flow, constant brew temperature, fixed bed geometry, homogeneous initial porosity, independent individual component transport. Results cannot establish coffee-to-coffee/setup calibration or member five-taste outcomes. Appendix 4.5 explicitly says **calculated final-cup concentrations were not compared against measured final-cup data**. Section 3.6 Table 8 R² .57/.80/.99/.90 under alternative dispersion configurations are model-fitting cases, NOT consumer accuracy or additional independent experiment repetitions.
