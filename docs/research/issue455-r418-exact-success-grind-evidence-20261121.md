## Reproducible source-level discrepancy (NOT full application/browser reproduction)

1. Existing `model.createSetup`/`recalibrate` accept fractional range `[0.15,2.55]`, and R417 already protects exact original measured shot precision. Consider a legitimately logged five-green Turbo shot with grinder `0.15`, original calibration `[0.15,2.55]`, same coffee, basket, style, Standard feed and current version.
2. `grindGuidance` filters that valid shot into `basketHits`, then unconditionally returns `{value:round(quantile(basketHits.map(sh=>sh.grindSetting),.5)),source:'Your successful setting',proven:true}` (engine ~208). Existing `round` rounds to **tenths**; one raw measured success `0.15` therefore becomes **0.2** while being called *Your successful setting* and `proven:true`. Nothing establishes 0.2 as an actually successful setting. `recommendation` consumes that guidance and its final grind normalization does not restore the original value.
3. A second independent example has real successful grinds `0.2` and `0.4`: their median `0.3` was never actually observed, yet retains the same provenance label. A single actual `0.2` correctly remains `0.2` / proven true as a positive control. A median `0.25` from `0.2/0.3` rounds to observed `0.3` but is *not* the exact measured median, another important provenance edge.
4. Verified exact source expressions and these four arithmetic fixtures in JS against immutable code-derived expressions. This is **source/arithmetic evidence**, not executed real module, DOM, device, member, empirical taste outcome, Node22 suite, or CI. No production incidence is asserted.
