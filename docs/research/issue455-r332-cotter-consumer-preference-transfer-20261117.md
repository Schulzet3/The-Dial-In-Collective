## Verifiable claims, exact locators and inference limits

1. **Protocol and measured constructs — Dryad Methods and Usage notes; publisher Abstract.** A medium-roast washed Honduran coffee was prepared in a commercial **drip brewer**, using a 3 × 3 × 3 target factorial (87/90/93°C brewing temperature, 1/1.25/1.5% beverage TDS, 16/20/24% extraction yield). Reported 118 mostly college-age black-coffee consumers evaluated 27 conditions. The questionnaire used a *nine-point hedonic overall-liking* score, four *five-point just-about-right* ratings (temperature, intensity, acidity and mouthfeel), and 17 check-all-that-apply descriptors. DIC instead uses **five distinct explicit five-point espresso taste inputs** for extraction, body, clarity, sweetness and astringency/dryness. Numeric similarity of scale length does not make constructs exchangeable.
2. **Segment result — original publisher Abstract; Supporting Information descriptions.** The paper reports two consumer segments most differentiated by brew strength/TDS, with dome- and saddle-like preference response surfaces. The publisher's supplement index describes 51 and 67 members in the respective clusters. These are **authors' aggregate published claims**; model specifications, figure data, individual prediction, robustness and external generalizability were **not independently verified**. They do not classify the app's member or establish a universal espresso recipe/temperature/grind optimum.
3. **Data-accounting caveat — Dryad Abstract/Methods.** The deposit describes 118 × 27 = **3,186** tastings but separately claims **3,168** spreadsheet rows. The 18-row discrepancy is a metadata observation, **not** proof of absent responses, improper analysis or a corrected count. Raw CSV and README not inspected; do not assert why counts differ. A single coffee, drip brewing, laboratory repeated tasting, demographic/sample limitations and group clustering cannot establish same-member single-control-change → next espresso → five taste ratings effectiveness.
