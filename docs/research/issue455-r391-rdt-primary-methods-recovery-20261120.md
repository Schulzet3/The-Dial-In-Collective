## Newly verified atomic claims (supersedes only R388's method-access gaps)
| Original claim | First-party study evidence and exact regime | Grade / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RDT can reduce electrostatic charge | Figure 2C / text: pregrind water at **20–30 microliters per gram** reduced measured charge-to-mass by **at least 50–60%** in the authors' sampled coffees. | **B:** directly measured, context-specific. Not universal across grinders, roast, ambient humidity or dosing. |
| RDT reduces grinder retention | Figure 4A/D / text: **10 microliters per gram** treatment yielded approximately **2.5% retained** versus untreated/high-voltage-ionized approximately **12%** in one EK43 dark-coffee comparison. Figure 4 ground-PSD cases were triplicated. | **B:** measured physical retention. Does not establish next-shot taste or universal retained-dose correction. |
| Wetting changes dark-coffee espresso flow/concentration | Figure 5 / STAR Methods: dark-roasted **Temascaltepec**, EK43 (98-mm stock burrs), **18.0 g in / 45.0 g out**, 196-N fixed tamp, **94 C**, **7 bar** static pressure, **2-second preinfusion**, Victoria Arduino Black Eagle, espresso grinder setting 1.0 arbitrary. RDT produced slower/lower flow and about **16% higher beverage concentration** under this one regime. | **B** for within-regime response, **D** for universal espresso or all-style advice. The paper does **not** measure DIC's five taste scores or comparative cup liking. A concentration change is not by itself a validated flavor-improvement claim. |
| Ionization vs wetting equivalence | Ionizer control did not reproduce RDT retention and flow response. Observed TDS **7.76% to 8.02%** in five-replicate comparison; Figure 5B reports treatment not statistically distinguishable at 0.05, versus a significant water condition in its tested comparison. | **B** limited evidence. Do not interpret unpaired figure numbers as a calibrated DIC correction or infer equivalence of methods. |
| Light roast inherits dark-roast effects | Authors report no statistically significant espresso-brewing changes in their light-roasted Temascaltepec comparison (supplement Figure S3). | **B/uncertain**: primary narrative checked, supplement itself **not independently inspected**. Do not generalize dark-roast magnitude to light or vice versa. |
| 196-N tamp and RDT are sensory optima | Tamp was **controlled**, not randomized against other forces. Wetting and ionizer experiments measured physical outcomes; no five-dimension palate rating or blinded flavor-benefit trial. | **D/unsupported** universal setting, sweetness, success or next-shot benefit. |

**Method qualifiers:** The study used two Mexican and one Ethiopian coffee, in-house dark/light roasts, EK43 stock 98-mm burrs, approximately 20 ± 3 C / 35 ± 7% RH, Faraday cup Q/m readings and Mastersizer PSD. Water distribution was not necessarily complete. Authors flag potential caking/corrosion at excessive wetting, context-dependent values, and no condensation *observed in their limited apparatus* (not a universal safety guarantee). The same paper describes raw data at DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.24737700; **raw dataset was NOT reviewed**.
