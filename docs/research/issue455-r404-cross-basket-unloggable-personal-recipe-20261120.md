## Narrow source-derived edge case, not an executed app test

Consider a synthetic, original, all-five-green Lungo shot with **dose 18g / measured yield 108g / measured time 36s** (ratio 1:6), original and current grinder reading within its recorded calibration, real timestamp, matching coffee, setup, feed mode and style, but **basket A**. All physical Log Shot measurement bounds are met; the unusually long ratio is the member's historical choice, *not* a recommended target. For a newly selected **basket B with valid dose 40g** on the same setup:
1. `familyEvidenceShots` admits that original shot and `chooseRecipeFamily` can select that successful family with a lower hardware-compatibility tier (same version, same feed/calibration, different basket).
2. `recommendation()` uses `ratioEvidence=feedLocal` when `basketFeed` is empty, retains measured 108/18 = **6** as the family center, and multiplies by **the currently selected 40g dose**, yielding a proposed center of **240g**.
3. Its current `rec.yield` finalization clamps interval endpoints **only if the center already lies within** 5–200g. Thus a 240g projected center bypasses the existing bound and can reach the recipe card, while Log Shot saving 240g would violate `validateShot`'s preexisting 200g maximum.

This is a deterministic source-path risk with arithmetic verified against the three immutable module blobs, **NOT** a claim that actual Node22 or browser reproduction ran, a live member hit it, any test failed, or a particular espresso ratio tastes good. The exact original 18g/108g success must stay intact in private History; one basket cannot establish another basket's espresso quality.
