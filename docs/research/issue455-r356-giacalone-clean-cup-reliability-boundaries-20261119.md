## Claim audit and uncertainty

1. **Not espresso; limited sample diversity** (accepted manuscript §§2.1–2.3): Nordic Roaster Forum **49 coffee professionals in 2016, 45 in 2017** evaluated respectively **three or four roasting variants of one washed Colombian Arabica**. This was coarse-ground **French press**, 50 g grounds/900 mL at 92 °C for 4 min, served at 55 °C. Each sample was blindly randomized and tasted **three times within one approximately 45-minute session**; this is **not** independent day-by-day espresso or personal before/after shot follow-up.
2. **Low cross-person agreement for one subjective score** (Results §3/Table 3): two-way inter-rater ICC for **“clean cup” = 0.01 (2016) and 0.13 (2017)**, with separately reported effects of assessor and replicate. The paper's authors interpret this as limited expert concept alignment. The scale is a continuous-style anchored attribute response and **not DIC's five ordinal 1–5 outcomes**, and the results do not tell us the reliability of DIC's own five-input interface.
3. **Do not treat non-significant effects as equivalence** (Methods §2.4, Results §3, Table 2): replicate ANOVA p values **0.198 and 0.789**, replicate×assessor **0.892 and 0.999**; the authors interpret these as high within-assessor reproducibility. Absence of statistical significance is **not** a direct equivalence margin, calibrated intra-rater ICC, or across-day retest proof. Accepted manuscript Conclusions §4 additionally writes **“ICC > 0.13”**, inconsistent with its own Results (**0.01 and 0.13**); numerical results take precedence in this note until Version-of-Record parity is checked. This is a source wording inconsistency **only**, not an assertion of misconduct or of a published corrigendum.
