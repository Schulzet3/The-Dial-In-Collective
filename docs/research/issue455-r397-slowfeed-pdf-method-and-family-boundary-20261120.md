## Primary-source correction / bounded new methods check

Directly read the original [Kaffeemacher Slow Feeding report](https://kaffeemacher.de/en/blogs/kaffeewissen/slow-feeding), Methods and Sensory Results: DF64 experiments across two test series, four tasted coffees, **two descriptive tastings** using an eight-attribute local score sheet; authors explicitly request later verification and larger blind panels. They report **mixed results**: one coffee rated higher with Slow Feed, two named examples did not improve and one was difficult to dial in. This contradicts any universal better-cup assertion; clarity/body descriptors are tentative, setup-specific, and **not interchangeable with DIC's five ratings**. Full raw sensory data, randomization/blinding and generalization across grinders not verified. Original [Smrke/Eiermann/Yeretzian 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-55831-x), Materials and Methods: 20 g espresso, Bentwood grinder, sieved-fines additions with replicate physical measurements; **does not manipulate feed mode**, and authors explicitly state the single Q-grader sensory exercise was not a systematic double-blind test. Peer-reviewed support for fines/permeability is not proof that Slow Feed improves taste or yields a universal numeric conversion.
