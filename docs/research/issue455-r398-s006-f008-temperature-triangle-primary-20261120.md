- **The actual experiment:** 24 assessors (20 women, four men) took two triangle tests (one odd cup among three), comparing brewing 80 °C versus **128 °C**, and 80 °C versus **93 °C**, respectively. Same study participants; one medium-dark Arabica/Canephora blend on ECM Synchronika, decalcified tap water, approximately 7 ± 0.5 g dose, 25 ± 2.5 g beverage, 25 ± 5 s extraction. Samples were served at **approximately 55 °C**, with blue-light/color and lid precautions against visual clues. Batches were made sequentially, equilibrated and held warm; preparation timing and volatile-loss variation remain confounders.
- **Direct published findings:** 80-versus-93 °C: **11/24** correctly identified the odd cup, under paper's **13/24** critical threshold for `alpha=0.05` (table states nonsignificant `alpha=0.20`). 80-versus-128 °C: **15/24**, above threshold (reported significance `alpha=0.01`). This verifies only the study's discrimination outcomes; **absence of significance is NOT temperature equivalence**. The 128 °C setpoint is not ordinary machine/user range or an applicable DIC espresso target.
- **Limits and contested inference:** It did not assess DIC's five independent taste-scale outcomes, within-coffee longitudinal preference, all roast/style categories, calibrated shot-to-shot prediction, or health outcomes. Authors discuss consumption-temperature risk, but this design **does not measure reduction in esophageal cancer risk** or authorize any app health claim or automatic lower-temperature policy. Contrast the already separately reviewed [Salamanca et al. 2017 S005/F007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27507518/), which measured temperature-profile-dependent chemistry/sensory response in other coffee and settings. Grading: **B (bounded pilot discrimination result), C (possible qualitative setup-dependent response), D/UNSUPPORTED (universal espresso optimal temperature, equal taste, guaranteed improvement, disease-risk reduction)**.
